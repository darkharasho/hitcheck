# M2 gate: zero-shot DINOv2 retrieval evaluation

**Date:** 2026-08-10
**Command:** `uv run python -m hitcheck_trainer.eval.synthetic`
**Gallery:** 20,427 catalog images embedded with `facebook/dinov2-base` (CLS token,
768-dim, L2-normalised), indexed with hnswlib (cosine space). 52 of 20,479
catalog cards have no image on disk (permanent upstream 404s) and are excluded.

## What this measures, and what it does not

This is a **synthetic upper bound**. The gallery is built from clean catalog
scans, and it is queried with those *same* images run through a seeded
degradation pipeline (perspective warp, glare, directional motion blur, JPEG
recompression — see `trainer/hitcheck_trainer/augment/degrade.py`). Real
stream frames add background clutter, an imperfect crop, the seller's hand in
frame, and camera framing/focus this pipeline does not simulate. **A poor
result here is decisive. A good result would only be permission to proceed to
M3 with real frames — not proof the problem is solved.**

## Method

- Gallery: all 20,427 available catalog images, embedded once and indexed
  (`data/index/cards.bin`, `hnswlib`, cosine space). Embedding was done in
  chunks of 256 decoded images at a time (never the whole catalog in memory
  at once — see `embed_in_chunks` in `synthetic.py`).
- Queries: an evenly-spaced sample of gallery ids, each degraded with
  `degrade(image, seed=<index>, strength=S)` and embedded the same way, then
  queried against the gallery index for its top-5 nearest neighbours by
  cosine distance.
- Query embedding reused the already-built gallery index (`--reuse-index`)
  for every run reported below — the expensive full-catalog embed ran once.
- Sample sizes: 500 at strength 1.0 (the brief's default), 300 at strengths
  0.0, 0.5 and 1.5 (the sweep).

## Results

| strength | n queries | top-1 | top-5 | mean top-1 distance | verdict |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 300 | 1.000 | 1.000 | 0.0000 | SKIP_TRAINING |
| 0.5 | 300 | 0.293 | 0.427 | 0.4017 | TRAIN_REQUIRED |
| 1.0 | 500 | 0.052 | 0.096 | 0.5311 | TRAIN_REQUIRED |
| 1.5 | 300 | 0.003 | 0.017 | 0.5835 | TRAIN_REQUIRED |

Strength 1.0 was independently re-run twice (once building the gallery fresh,
once reusing the saved index) and produced the identical top1=0.052/top5=0.096
result both times — this is a stable, reproducible measurement, not noise.

Strength 0.0 (no degradation at all — the query is pixel-identical to its
gallery entry after the identity no-op in `degrade()`) scores a perfect
1.000/1.000, which confirms the retrieval pipeline itself (embedding, index
build, index query, scoring) is wired correctly; the collapse at strength 0.5
and above is a property of the degradation-vs-retrieval combination, not a
bug in the harness.

## Verdict at strength 1.0

**top1 = 0.052**, far below the 0.70 `TRAIN_REQUIRED` boundary.

**Interpretation guidance** (from the task brief):
- top-1 ≥ 0.90 at strength 1.0 → `SKIP_TRAINING`
- top-1 0.70–0.90 → borderline (check whether misses are reprints; OCR
  tiebreaker may close the gap)
- top-1 < 0.70 → `TRAIN_REQUIRED`

**Call: `TRAIN_REQUIRED`.** This is not a borderline result — it is not even
close. Off-the-shelf DINOv2 CLS-token retrieval, at the degradation level
meant to model real stream capture, does not identify cards reliably enough
to ship as-is.

## Reprints vs. unrelated cards

**All 15 misses above are genuinely unrelated cards** — different Pokémon,
different sets, different eras of card design. None of them is a
near-duplicate reprint of the true card (the kind of case the spec's
set-number OCR tiebreaker is meant for). This was checked directly against
the database, not inferred from ids.

Two things stand out in the full 474-miss list (not just the 15 above):

1. **No reprint pattern.** Scanning further into the failures, the same
   picture holds — predictions land on cards from unrelated sets, unrelated
   Pokémon, unrelated eras. This rules out the optimistic read of a low
   top-1 score (i.e., "it's just confusing near-identical reprints, OCR will
   fix it"). It isn't that. At this degradation level the embedding is not
   discriminating between cards at all.
2. **A recurring "attractor" prediction.** `neo1-30` (Clefairy, Neo Genesis)
   appears as the predicted card for several unrelated true cards
   (base1-43, base3-40, base4-29, base6-71, basep-18, and others further
   into the failure list). This is consistent with what heavy JPEG
   recompression plus perspective warp plus glare does to a CLS-token global
   descriptor: multiple degraded queries converge toward a generic,
   low-information region of embedding space near whichever gallery image
   happens to sit closest to that region, rather than toward their own true
   embedding. That is a structural weakness of holistic (single global
   vector) DINOv2 retrieval under heavy degradation, not a labeling or
   database problem.

The strength=0.5 sweep point (top1=0.293) shows the same pattern of
essentially unrelated cards, though slightly less extreme. Retrieval quality
falls off a cliff between strength 0.0 (perfect) and strength 0.5 (already
below the 0.70 floor), and continues to degrade further, not plateau, out to
strength 1.5.

## Decision: TRAIN_REQUIRED

Off-the-shelf DINOv2 zero-shot retrieval does **not** clear the bar, even
under this optimistic synthetic setup where the gallery and the query are
literally the same scan. Since this is measured against the *best case*
(no background clutter, no bad crop, no seller's hand, no camera focus
issues — only the four degradations `degrade()` simulates), a worse result
should be expected on real stream frames, not a better one.

**What M3 looks like as a result:**

- M7's planned ArcFace fine-tune is back on the roadmap, not dropped. This
  eval's query/gallery pairs (with known ground truth ids) become its
  validation set — the same 20,427-card gallery, the same seeded
  degradation pipeline, so future fine-tuned-model runs are directly
  comparable to this baseline.
- M3 should not block on the fine-tune landing first. Proceed with capture,
  the stability gate, and the detection path using the current zero-shot
  embeddings as a placeholder identification backend, but do not treat its
  output as trustworthy for a purchase decision — the confidence gate
  (spec) should be tuned assuming the underlying retrieval is currently
  weak, and M3 real-frame testing should specifically check whether
  real-frame accuracy is even worse than this synthetic floor, which would
  make the ArcFace fine-tune urgent rather than optional.
- Do not ship a build that surfaces raw top-1 DINOv2 identification to the
  user as a priced result. At today's zero-shot accuracy this would be
  wrong roughly 19 times out of 20 at strength-1.0-equivalent degradation.

## Reproducibility notes

- Peak resident memory observed during the full-gallery embed (initial run,
  gallery + 500-query embed in one process) was **1.81 GB** (`/usr/bin/time
  -v` maximum resident set size = 1,809,412 KB), well under the ~2 GB
  caution threshold and nowhere near the 4.5 GB the whole-catalog-in-memory
  approach this evaluation deliberately avoids would have used. Chunked
  loading (`load_chunk`/`embed_in_chunks`, `--chunk 256`) never holds more
  than one chunk of decoded images at a time; the 20,427 × 768 float32
  embedding matrix (~62 MB) and the hnswlib index (68 MB on disk) are the
  only things accumulated for the full catalog.
- All sweep runs after the first reused the already-built gallery index
  (`--reuse-index`), so only a few hundred query images were embedded per
  run, not the full catalog.
- GPU: RTX 4070 Ti, `cuda` device confirmed in every run's log line
  (`embedding gallery on cuda (dim 768)`).

## Controller addendum — the TRAIN_REQUIRED verdict is NOT trustworthy

Added after reviewing the run. The verdict above is an artifact of an
uncalibrated degradation scale, not a finding about DINOv2.

### The strength scale was never calibrated against a real stream

Actual parameters at each strength, computed from `augment/degrade.py`:

| strength | JPEG quality | blur kernel | corner jitter | glare peak |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0.1 | 56 | 4px | ±3px | +19 |
| 0.2 | 51 | 5px | ±6px | +21 |
| 0.5 | 37 | 9px | ±14px | +52 |
| **1.0** | **15** | **15px** | **±29px** | **+105** |

Strength 1.0 stacks JPEG quality 15 (near-unrecognisable — real H.264 streams
sit around an effective quality of 70-85), a 15px directional smear across a
240px-wide image, a 12% perspective jitter, and a glare blob adding +105
brightness over a large ellipse. That is not "a card held up on a stream";
that is a destroyed image. Measuring retrieval on it answers a question
nobody asked.

The "attractor" behaviour the run observed — many unrelated true cards all
predicting `neo1-30` — is the classic signature of inputs pushed past the
information floor, where embeddings collapse toward a generic region of the
space. It is evidence the inputs carry no signal, not evidence that DINOv2
confuses similar cards.

### The full curve, including low strengths measured afterwards

| strength | top-1 | top-5 |
|---|---|---|
| 0.0 | 1.000 | 1.000 |
| 0.1 | 0.870 | 0.950 |
| 0.2 | 0.750 | 0.860 |
| 0.3 | 0.540 | 0.650 |
| 0.5 | 0.293 | 0.427 |
| 1.0 | 0.052 | 0.096 |
| 1.5 | 0.003 | 0.017 |

There is a steep but smooth cliff between 0.1 and 0.3. Where a real eBay Live
or TikTok Live frame sits on this axis is unknown — and it is the only thing
that determines the answer.

### What IS established

- **The harness is correct.** Strength 0.0 returns a perfect 1.000 top-1 across
  20,427 gallery cards. Embedder, index, id mapping, degradation plumbing and
  scoring all work end to end. That is worth having.
- **Memory behaviour is fixed.** Peak RSS 1.81GB for the full gallery embed,
  versus the 4.52GB whole-catalog-in-memory design that contributed to an OOM.
- **DINOv2 is not obviously insufficient.** At mild degradation it retrieves
  87% top-1 / 95% top-5 from a 20k-card gallery with no training at all.

### The decision is BLOCKED, not made

`TRAIN_REQUIRED` should not be acted on. The next step is calibration, not
training:

1. Capture real frames from an actual eBay Live / TikTok Live stream — this
   needs the M0 capture path, which is built but still unverified by a human.
2. Measure their actual JPEG-equivalent quality, motion blur and perspective,
   and locate reality on the strength axis.
3. Re-read the curve at that point. If real frames land near 0.1-0.2, top-5 is
   86-95% and a set-number OCR tiebreaker over the top-5 candidates may well be
   enough to ship without training anything.

The spec called the augmentation pipeline "the real asset" that determines
whether any of this works. It was built on guessed severity and never checked
against a real frame. That is the gap to close before spending months on
training.
